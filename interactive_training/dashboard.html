NLL (train & test)i Negative log-likelihood per weighted token.
  • train NLL (train_mean_nll) — weighted mean NLL on each training batch.
  • test NLL (test/nll, dotted) — NLL on held-out test split, computed via forward pass on eval steps.
Train NLL should decrease monotonically. If test NLL bottoms out and starts rising while train NLL keeps falling, you're overfitting; the gap between the two curves is your generalization error.

Reward & accuracyi Six lines vs. training step. Train (solid) and eval (no marker style change here, but eval keys are test/...) for three signals:
  • reward (env/all/reward/total) — mean total reward per rollout (correctness + format).
  • correct (env/all/correct) — fraction of rollouts with the right final answer.
  • format (env/all/format, dotted) — fraction obeying the answer schema.
Train correct should rise. If it climbs while eval correct stalls or drops → overfitting / reward hacking. Format should pin near 1.0; sudden dips signal schema breakage.

Optim: grad norm · entropy · LRi Three independent y-axes (left, right, far-right):
  • grad_norm (skyrl.ai/grad_norm) — pre-clip policy gradient L2. Stable is good; spikes = unstable update (often co-occur with KL spikes).
  • entropy (optim/entropy, RL only) — mean per-token policy entropy on sampled tokens. Falls as the model commits; falling too fast → mode collapse and loss of rollout diversity.
  • lr (optim/lr for RL, skyrl.ai/learning_rate for SFT, dotted) — actual LR after warmup/decay. Confirms the schedule.

KL (sample vs train)i KL divergence between the policy that sampled the rollouts and the policy after the gradient step — the "how big a step did we take" gauge.
  • kl v1 (optim/kl_sample_train_v1) — log-ratio estimator.
  • kl v2 (optim/kl_sample_train_v2) — (r-1) − log r, lower variance, always ≥ 0.
Should be small and roughly flat. Spikes typically precede a reward/correctness dip on the next step. Large gap between v1 and v2 ⇒ heavy outlier importance ratios in the batch.

Group composition (train)i Stacked %. For each prompt, GRPO samples group_size completions and labels the group:
  • all-good — every completion correct.
  • mixed — some right, some wrong; only these produce useful advantages.
  • all-bad — every completion wrong.
Early on, all-bad dominates. The mixed band growing is the signal that learning is happening. When all-good takes over, the curriculum is too easy — advantages collapse to zero and reward stops moving.

Token counts per turni Mean tokens per turn, train (solid) and eval (dotted):
  • action tok (env/all/ac_tokens_per_turn) — tokens the model generated (completion length).
  • obs tok (env/all/ob_tokens_per_turn) — observation tokens fed back per turn (prompt / tool output). Roughly constant for single-turn tasks like GSM8K.
Rising action-tok often means longer chains-of-thought. Hitting the max_tokens ceiling silently caps reward — bump max_tokens or shorten prompts. Train vs eval divergence hints at distribution mismatch.

Completion truncationi Fraction of turns whose completion hit (or nearly hit) the max_tokens cap, train (solid) and eval (dotted):
  • at max (env/all/ac_tokens_frac_at_max) — completions within 4 tokens of the cap, i.e. almost certainly truncated mid-generation.
  • near max (env/all/ac_tokens_frac_near_max) — completions using ≥ 95% of the cap; the population at risk of truncation.
A non-trivial at-max fraction means rewards are being silently capped by truncation, not by the policy. Raise max_tokens (or shorten prompts) until this falls back toward zero. Only emitted when max_tokens is known.

Step timing breakdowni Stacked seconds per step:
  • run_evals — eval rollouts (only on eval steps; the periodic tall bars).
  • assemble_training_data — sampling + grading the train rollouts.
  • train — forward / backward / optimizer step.
  • compute_kl_sample_train — extra forward pass to measure post-update KL.
  • save_checkpoint — checkpoint write (only on save steps).
For hosted RL, assemble usually dominates (rollouts). If train balloons you're optimizer-bound; if assemble balloons it's sampling throughput. Tune eval_every / save_every based on their bar heights.

Forward/backward chunkingi Parsed from logs.log lines emitted by the Azure finetuning SDK: [forward_backward_post] batch of N datums split into K chunks: [...].
  • chunks/call — how many micro-batches the SDK split each forward_backward request into.
  • max / min chunk size — largest and smallest chunk in each call (in datums).
  • last split — the exact split sizes for the most recent step.
Each step normally produces 1–2 chunks. A sudden jump to many chunks (or a tiny tail chunk like [98, 96, 6]) means the SDK hit a token budget and had to fragment the batch — expect that step's time/train to spike. Persistent multi-chunking ⇒ your effective batch is too large for the server's per-request budget.

Per-iteration artifactsi Self-contained HTML logs written by the trainer for each iteration:
  • train — sampled trajectory groups for the training rollouts (prompt, completions, rewards, group composition).
  • eval — same view for the held-out eval rollouts (only on iterations where eval ran).
Open one to inspect actual model outputs at that step — the fastest way to see what the model is doing when a metric moves.
